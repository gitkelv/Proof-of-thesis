      [ Method ]

      What happens every morning

      1. Fetch

        At 06:17 UTC a GitHub Action pulls Intents volume, fees, revenue and TVL from DefiLlama, plus stablecoin supply and price. Total supply and inflation come straight from a NEAR RPC node.

      2. Measure

        One plain JavaScript file, model/thesis.mjs, turns raw series into metrics. The same file runs in CI, in the tests and in your browser.

      3. Grade

        Each claim is graded holding, weakening or broken against fixed thresholds. Two broken claims and the verdict becomes "Thesis broken".

      4. Publish

        The grade is committed to the repo, added to the append-only ledger and deployed to this page.

      Kill criteria

      Every threshold lives in one list in model/thesis.mjs. Disagree with one? Change it and rerun.

      [ Questions ]

      Before you ask

      Is this investment advice?

      No. It's an open model with its assumptions in plain sight. It shows which parts of a common NEAR bull case today's data supports and which it doesn't. Do your own research.

      Why would a thesis page admit it's weak?

      A thesis that can't fail tells you nothing. Writing the kill line down in advance is what separates analysis from marketing. If the buyback claim is weak today, the page says so and shows how much growth would fix it.

      I disagree with a threshold. What do I do?

      Edit the CLAIMS list in model/thesis.mjs and run npm run live, and every grade updates. Or move the sliders under "Your numbers" to test your own growth and capture assumptions.

      What does "Rerun it in your browser" do?

      It downloads fresh data from DefiLlama and a NEAR RPC node into your browser and runs the same model file there. No server of ours sits in between, so you can check that the published grades match.

      What are the known gaps?

      Buybacks use DefiLlama's holders-revenue series. Issuance uses the onchain maximum inflation rate, which slightly overstates real issuance when validators miss blocks. Past supply is estimated by rolling today's onchain supply back at the inflation rate. Projections hold the NEAR price flat.
